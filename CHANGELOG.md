# Changelog

## 3.3

### Added

- **Panels now have 76 more visual choices.** Volume gains six styles and six layouts; the progress ring gains eight styles plus a new nine-choice layout axis; seek gains eight readout styles and seven meter layouts; Quick actions gains six styles and seven arrangements; Queue gains eight row styles; and the shared panel background gains eleven treatments. Every choice is rendered independently on the watch and in the phone preview, is saved per face where appropriate, and participates in theme export/import and Community-theme validation.
- **Quick actions can be assigned directly to a watch button or gesture.** The new **Open quick actions panel** action opens the Panel immediately, so users no longer need to double-tap the centre of the screen.
- **Two new watch faces: Ribbon and Frame.** Ribbon frames the current cover with four tall queue-art rails, read as a timeline the way Carousel's is — what already played to the left, what is coming to the right — and the covers are sent at the larger size those rails need instead of the 30dp list thumbnail. Frame places the artist chip, the title and a wide cover inside a rounded card that takes its colour from the album, with the cover keeping the same margin from the card on every side and the chip's label picking black or white by measured contrast against whatever colour the record produced. Neither face carries a fixed colour of its own: the artist line follows the music like everywhere else. Both are available from the watch-face picker and the phone's live preview, both confirm a centre tap with the play/pause glyph — they have no visible transport control to change shape — and both take the album-tinted volume, progress, quick-panel and queue surfaces so an overlay opening over them belongs to the same composition. Each also carries its own progress indicator, since both ship with the edge ring off: a hairline under Ribbon's centre cover, and a bar along the bottom edge of Frame's, both following the Progress ring colour and both switchable off from **Layout progress indicator** like every other layout that draws one. Frame's cover takes a corner radius concentric with the card holding it, so the two curves read as parallel instead of the cover looking like a rectangle dropped into a rounded box. Frame shows the elapsed and total time opposite its artist chip, where a notification puts its timestamp. Ribbon's title and artist are now inset for the round glass at the depth they actually sit rather than by a flat side padding, so a long title stops before the bezel instead of running under it.
- **The Note layout can reshape its cover.** Note draws the track as a small cover beside one line of text, and that cover was always a circle — the contact-photo look the face is built around, but a shape you could not change even when a square sleeve suited the record better. **Cover shape** now sits on the Player page whenever Note is selected, offering the same four silhouettes the Carousel cards already use: rounded, square, squircle or circle. It is per layout and per theme like every other appearance setting, it shows in the phone's live preview, and it is its own setting rather than a shared one — a rail of cards and a thumbnail beside a sentence want opposite answers, so choosing here never reshapes Carousel.
- **Icon selection was redesigned, and it can now find what it's carrying.** Picking a built-in icon for an action or a button used to be one plain grid with no way to search it — already 163 icons deep, that meant scrolling to look for something by eye with no name shown anywhere. The dialog now opens with a search box, and every icon carries a visible name underneath it (accent- and case-insensitive, so "assao"/"ação" and "lyrics"/"Lyrics" all match). It also carries more to find, 235 in total: the **Lyrics** and **Open quick actions panel** action glyphs were missing despite both actions existing since they shipped, thirty more icons already used elsewhere in Settings and the Watch tab were never reachable here, Svartifoss's own mark is offered first so "use the app's icon" is one tap away, and thirty-nine new Material Symbols icons are now available throughout the app, including a set of layout glyphs (window panes, split panes, columns, tiles) for anyone building custom actions around Split, the mini-button rail or list density.
- **Title and artist can be forced to a text case.** **Title case** and **Artist case**, on the Text page, choose between Normal (whatever case the playing app reports), UPPERCASE and lowercase — independently for each line, since a title in small caps and an artist line left alone is a common combination. It reaches every face, applied last so it always wins over a face's own styling (Vinyl's designed all-caps title, for one) without disturbing anything else about it - weight, size, opacity and spacing stay exactly as set. Note, whose title and artist share one sentence rather than two separate lines, cases each half by its own setting before joining them, so choosing UPPERCASE for the title there does not also shout the artist.
- **Discover Community themes.** The Watch themes screen now opens an opt-in gallery of publicly published looks. Search by theme or author, filter by base layout, and order the downloaded public catalogue by newest or most liked — all locally, with no account. Gallery cards and moderation previews stay synthetic. Tapping a card opens a detail page before installation, with local Player, always-on, Volume, Progress, Quick panel, and Queue renders: their text, timing, and queue remain sample data, while the current album cover may appear only from memory on the phone. It is never uploaded, transmitted, captured as a screenshot, or paired with live title, artist, or playback data. Only **Add and apply** puts the theme in **My themes**, where it remains your own local copy.
- **Private Community-theme likes, with no account.** Tapping Like never asks you to sign in: the app creates an anonymous Firebase identity for the reaction silently, so liking, unliking and the private **Liked** filter all work without a Google account. That identity lives only in the app's local storage, so clearing app data means starting over with a clean slate — deliberately, because the counts are a popularity signal rather than a ballot, and demanding an account for a heart tap costs far more than the occasional duplicate. Signing in later to submit a theme upgrades that same identity in place, so reactions left beforehand are kept. Each identity still has at most one private reaction per theme; voters cannot be listed and no APK can change a public count. The trusted publisher aggregates reactions into the static catalogue, so a theme's total settles when that catalogue is next rewritten — immediately when a theme is published or withdrawn, and otherwise at most weekly, because rewriting the public file every day merely to move a popularity number would put a commit a day into an app's repository for no one's benefit. Until then a card and its detail page show the published figure moved by whatever you just did, so your own tap is never the thing you are waiting on.
- **Submit a Community theme.** Submitting is the one community action that asks who you are: a theme you created locally can be sent for review with Google Sign-In, a public theme name and a chosen author pseudonym; your Google name and email are not published. The submission needs at least twelve applicable visual changes, is limited to three submissions in a rolling 24-hour window, and becomes public only after a separate moderator review and trusted publication check.
- **One Community account, kept in Settings — and deletable, on your terms.** Signing in used to happen inside the submission form and feel like it happened every time, so the author name and the account picker were both part of sending a theme rather than something settled once. **Settings → General → Community themes → Community account** is now where that connection lives: sign in once, see which Google account is connected, and remove it from this device without touching anything stored elsewhere. Submitting reuses it silently from then on. The same screen can delete the account outright, which is the part the app could not honestly offer before — an APK can delete its own Firebase identity, but not the public catalogue in Git, and doing only the first would destroy the account while leaving its published themes standing with nobody able to ask for them back. So deletion is *requested* here and carried out by the same trusted publisher that publishes themes, which removes the public files in a commit first and the Firebase records after. Before it is sent, the app asks the one question nobody else can answer for you: **keep my published themes** leaves them in the gallery under the author name they were published with and strips your account from the record behind them, while **delete my published themes too** withdraws them and their likes on the next publication run. Either way your identity, your submission history and every like you left are deleted, and a theme still waiting for review is deleted under both — there is nothing published to keep. An account that exists only for likes can delete itself the same way and is not asked the question, since it owns no themes. It is one-way and normally completes within a day, and the screen says so before asking rather than after.
- **My submissions.** Sending a theme used to be write-only: it left the phone and the only way to learn what happened was to notice it appear in the gallery, which tells you nothing at all about one that was rejected. Community account now has **See my submissions**, listing everything this account has sent with where each one stands — waiting for review, approved, published, rejected, or being removed — and what that actually means for it. It reads only your own records; Firestore refuses anyone else's. It is reachable from the account screen and from the gallery's own toolbar, shows the public like count on anything you have published — so you can see whether a theme landed — and can **take one of your themes back down** without deleting your account. Removing is a request rather than an instant delete, for the same reason: the gallery is a set of files in Git, so the next publication run removes the file, the catalogue entry and the likes, and whoever already installed it keeps their own copy. It shows no reviewer, and that is what made the screen possible: the reviewer's identity moved out of the submission and into a moderator-only record, because Firestore grants access one document at a time and never one field at a time, so while the verdict and the name behind it shared a record, the only thing an author could be shown was their own still-pending queue.
- **Duplicate themes are refused before they are sent.** The published catalogue now carries each theme's settings fingerprint, so the submission screen can tell you that these exact settings are already published on this layout — before asking you to sign in, rather than after a day of waiting for a review that was always going to reject it. The check reads the catalogue the phone has already downloaded and deliberately fails open: a phone that cannot reach the gallery is not blocked from submitting, and the trusted publisher re-applies the same rule from the side that actually decides.
- **Themes can be submitted from the Community gallery itself.** Sending a theme for review used to be reachable only from its own overflow menu back in **My themes**, which is an odd place to look for it while you are standing in the gallery those themes end up in. The gallery now carries an upload button that lists your saved themes and takes up to three at once — the same three the rolling 24-hour limit allows, refused at the moment you pick a fourth rather than after its form has been filled in — then opens the ordinary submission screen for each in turn, since the public name and pseudonym belong to one theme at a time. Themes downloaded from the gallery are deliberately not offered: a copy has to be duplicated into a theme of your own before it can be submitted as yours.

### Changed
- **The Watch themes screen's community-gallery row carries a shopping-bag icon instead of a palette one.** A palette reads as "customize the look"; this row instead opens the public catalogue other people have published, which a storefront glyph represents more honestly.
- **List pill size is now Queue-only.** Changing it no longer resizes the action menu, quick panel or shortcuts; those surfaces keep their own normal rhythm.
- **A cover with no colour in it now produces one colour, not four.** A true black-and-white cover has no hue, and HSL has to return something for one — it returns 0°, which is red. Nothing chose that, and it was being amplified inconsistently: the artist line came out pure grey, the quick panel at a third saturation, the transport buttons at more, and the phone's palette dots at more again — one accent, four answers, on the same screen. That is because ninety-seven places clamp the accent up to one of thirteen different saturation floors before painting it, while the text path clamps only lightness and never touches saturation. The accent is now given a real colour once, where it is resolved, so every one of those floors has nothing left to do and the whole watch — text included — agrees with what **Colors from the music** shows. Covers that already have colour are untouched, a hand-picked Normal colour is never promoted, and the filled surfaces look essentially as they did, because the value they are promoted to is the one those surfaces were already inventing. Turning **Use a colour palette** off on such a cover also could not previously change anything at all: the three slots differed only in lightness, and every consumer overwrites lightness.

- **The Community account moved to the top of Settings → General.** It was at the bottom of Data & support, which is where you look for a backup, not for the account every other community action depends on.
- **The moderation page manages themes instead of only queueing them.** It lists submissions by status — pending, approved, published, rejected, withdrawn, or all — rather than showing the pending queue and nothing else, so an approval made by mistake is visible instead of vanishing until the next publication run. A decision can be reopened, a public name or author credit corrected while the theme is still not public, and a theme taken down at any point: taking one down only sets a status, and the publisher removes the file, the catalogue entry and the likes on its next run, because the gallery lives in Git and no browser page can reach it. Every action is written as one atomic batch that records who did it, so a verdict can never exist without an author.
- **Community themes is now a focused, adaptive gallery.** Large watch previews lead each card in a two-column grid on ordinary phones, expanding or collapsing with the available width and font size. Search sits above one quiet row of scrollable filters, titles and authors are left-aligned, every card carries its like count, and installed, update, compatibility, loading, empty and offline states share the same minimal Lyra hierarchy.
- **Community-theme credits can stay public-anonymous.** Submitting still uses Google privately for moderation and future ownership, but a new **Publish as Anonymous** choice hides the author pseudonym from the public gallery.
- **The Community themes search field keeps one colour.** Focusing it no longer repaints the whole box in the text colour; it stays the quiet surface it shows at rest, with only the cursor and selection tinted.
- **Built-in themes are safe starting points now.** Their overflow menu can duplicate the current built-in look into **My themes**, ready for independent editing. The risky **Apply this look to all faces** shortcut is gone, so one accidental tap can no longer overwrite every built-in face or flood the watch-settings snapshot with thousands of copied values.
- **Loading and selection now speak the app's own visual language.** Indeterminate waits in the phone's theme flows use the same three animated music bars as the watch instead of circular spinners. **Filter by layout**, the **Newest** sorting menu, and **Remove theme** now reuse the same rounded Lyra dialog, stock radio rows and live theme accent as **Accent floor**. An icon marks themes already saved locally without covering their previews with **IN MY THEMES** text.
- **Global colour choices come before per-element overrides.** The Colors page now starts with **Colors from the music**, ahead of title, artist and clock colours. Opening an already-downloaded Community theme now offers **Remove theme** instead of the misleading **Add and apply** action; removing an active theme safely returns the watch to its built-in base layout first.
- **Watch text editing is compact and contextual.** The Text page now works like a small document-formatting toolbar: choose the element you want to edit, then adjust only the controls that apply to it instead of scrolling through a long list of preference rows. The Community submission screen also follows the live app accent throughout, keeps its primary action readable over light colours, and vertically centres the public name and author fields. The Google Sans Flex axes are reachable from it again: its Flex button used to open a dialog that showed the explanatory line and none of the four axes, so width, optical size, grade and roundness could not be adjusted at all. Title and artist can now choose their own typeface as well; an explicit Flex choice for Title, Artist, Clock, Lyrics or track time gets its own width, optical size, grade and roundness controls, while following the global font continues to use the global axes.
- **Watch colour editing is compact and contextual too.** The Color page now works the way Text does: the palette every element inherits sits at the top — accent source, treatment, tone, colour shift and the palette switch, with the three colours it actually resolves to shown as live dots rather than named and left to be checked in the preview — and below it you pick an element and adjust only what applies to it. Title, artist, clock, progress bar, volume bar and Quick actions each get their colour mode, their own colour where that mode has one, and the readability correction where it means anything; the clock keeps its opacity beside them. Rows that were previously scattered across four stacked groups are now one surface, and a dot on each button shows what that element will actually be, including the two cases where the honest answer is that nothing has been decided yet: a title still using its face's own colour, and a clock set to follow the artwork beneath it. Nothing about the values changed — every control is still the same per-face preference, still searchable by name, and a search result lands on the right element with its control highlighted.
- **Watch panel editing is compact and contextual too.** Panel followed Text and Color: the backdrop and blur that every overlay shares sit at the top, and below them you pick a surface — Volume, Progress, Quick actions or Queue — and see only that surface's own controls instead of scrolling six stacked groups to find the two rows you wanted. The Progress tab keeps the resting ring and the seek overlay as separate things, because they are: one is on screen the whole time and the other only exists while you are dragging. Quick actions keeps its shortcut library, its panel source and the note explaining how the panel is actually opened, since that is the one place on the page that says so. Nothing about the values changed — the same per-face preferences, the same dialogs, the same search — and the two settings here that are deliberately not per-face (where the quick panel gets its actions, and whether queue covers are downloaded) stay global exactly as before.
- **The Player page's picker rows no longer draw a stray scrollbar.** The rows are built at bind time, so the sweep that clears scrollbars across the editor ran before they existed and never reached them; their text also overran the content box a Material button's default insets leave inside a 48dp row, which is what makes a button report something to scroll in the first place. The rows now clear the flag themselves, sit on zeroed insets like every other button in these editors, and set their label a point smaller so "Track time display · Always" fits without crowding.
- **The phone's playback-queue sheet was redesigned, and now shows covers.** Tapping the mini player opened a plain column of titles: no artwork, no sense of where you were in the session, and the playing track marked three times over — an accent bar, a tinted background and a bold accent title all saying the same thing. Every row now leads with the track's cover, the playing one is marked by the app's own three-bar animation (the same one the watch queue uses, so the mark means one thing on both screens), upcoming rows carry their position in the queue, and tracks already played recede instead of sitting there as equals. The sheet opens on what is playing rather than at the top, with a line of context above it, and its header says how long the queue actually is. Covers come from the same resolver and the same **Fetch queue covers online** switch the watch queue already used — nothing new is requested, and with that switch off the rows simply show a placeholder. Underneath, the list became a properly recycling one: the old version built and kept a view for every entry, which was merely wasteful for two lines of text and would have meant holding a couple of hundred decoded covers once artwork arrived.
- **The Player page was rebuilt around the face and what is on it.** It was the longest of the four and the one where most rows did not apply to the face you were on. The face now leads it, because it is the page's subject rather than one setting among many — it decides which of the rest even mean anything — with the control style beside it. Below that, the seven "is this on the screen" switches became a field of chips: the same question asked seven times, answered at a glance in a quarter of the height, and read as a set of things rather than as seven sentences each starting with "Show". Only genuine multi-way choices stay as rows, because a chip cannot express one. Deliberately no tab strip here, unlike Text, Color and Panel: those three apply one repeated set of controls to parallel subjects, so switching subject is the natural gesture, while this page describes a single screen and a rail would have been two tabs holding unrelated lists. Controls a face cannot use are simply absent — Carousel's card shape, Split's panel, Expressive's seek mode and Classic's tap flash appear for the face that has them, and the Metadata face's six content blocks get their own card, read straight from the registry that face draws from, so a block added later shows up on its own.
- **Reset all faces is gone from the Player page.** Wiping every face you have tuned sat one mistap away from "Reset this layout", permanently, for a job almost nobody does twice. The one place it is genuinely warranted still offers it: the one-time prompt shown after upgrading from 2.x, which explains why it is asking. Resetting the layout you are on is unchanged.
- **Track time is now a first-class Text element.** The `1:23 / 3:45` readout has its own typeface, weight, italic, size, opacity and spacing controls, scoped to the current face and mirrored in the phone preview. Leaving it on **Follow the design** retains each face's original numeric treatment. An explicit Google Sans Flex choice also has its own four axes.
- **The text-font catalog is much broader.** Sixty-one new bundled OFL families are available everywhere a typeface can be chosen, taking the picker from 37 options to 88 — 73 bundled faces plus the 15 the watch already provides. The first ten cover the everyday gaps — **Inter**, **Atkinson Hyperlegible**, **Rubik**, **Barlow Condensed**, **Oswald**, **Lora**, **Fraunces**, **Space Mono**, **Archivo Black** and **Dancing Script** — for accessible reading, compact long titles, editorial serif, technical mono, display and handwriting. The rest widen every one of those directions: geometric and humanist sans (**DM Sans**, **Jost**, **Manrope**, **Outfit**, **Plus Jakarta Sans**, **Quicksand**, **Varela Round**, **Cabin**, **Mulish**, **Nunito**, **Lexend**, **Chivo**, **Alata**, **ABeeZee**, **Josefin Sans**), book and slab serifs (**Crimson Pro**, **Domine**, **Zilla Slab**, **Aleo**, **Arvo**, **Bree Serif**, **Crete Round**), monospace for code-like readouts (**Fira Code**, **Inconsolata**, **Roboto Mono**, **Source Code Pro**, **Courier Prime**), condensed and technical faces (**Anton**, **Staatliches**, **Teko**, **Rajdhani**, **Yanone Kaffeesatz**, **Titillium Web**, **Exo 2**, **Play**, **Oxanium**, **Kanit**, **Russo One**), and display and hand faces for a face built around one word (**Abril Fatface**, **Alfa Slab One**, **Lobster**, **Righteous**, **Shrikhand**, **Rowdies**, **Bangers**, **Acme**, **Black Ops One**, **Amatic SC**, **Indie Flower**, **Press Start 2P**, **Silkscreen**). All work offline on the watch and are available to saved and Community themes. They do make both apps noticeably larger — the fonts themselves are about 20 MB in each APK — which is the trade for having every one of them on the wrist with no network.
- **Streaming shortcuts are one place in the action picker.** The liked-songs and shuffled-liked-songs actions no longer crowd the root list; they live alongside the other streaming shortcuts, while already configured actions keep working unchanged.
- **The Watch appearance pages lost a stray heading and a stray scrollbar.** The Text page opened with the word “Text” directly beneath the tab already named Text, so the first thing on the page was a label repeating its own name. And a scrollbar was being drawn along the formatting row, where nothing is meant to scroll: not by the row itself, which never had one, but by each button carrying an icon — an icon leaves a button's content taller than its insets allow, so the button reports something to scroll and paints a thumb at the edge of its own content. Buttons in this editor no longer draw scrollbars at all. Its two cards also sit on a softer 12dp corner instead of 18dp.
- **The Play button stays off the Watch tab.** With nothing playing, the floating Play button appeared on every tab that has no button of its own — including Watch, where it parked over the bottom-right corner of the appearance controls being edited. Starting playback is not what that screen is for, so it no longer appears there at all.

### Fixed

- **Themes built on the Ribbon and Frame layouts could not be submitted to the gallery at all.** The server keeps its own list of which layouts a submission may use, and the two faces added in this release were never added to it. Everything on the phone accepted them — the theme validated, the preview drew it, the originality count passed — and the submission was then refused at the last step, reported as "Couldn't submit this theme. Check your connection and try again" on a connection that was fine. Both layouts are accepted now, and the check that guards that list is no longer written out by hand: it reads the app's own layout registry, so a future face cannot be added to the app and forgotten on the server.
- **Submitting a second theme from the same account always failed.** The rules that guard a submission are evaluated under a fixed complexity budget, and the checks around the new author-name reservation pushed an ordinary submission past it. The first submission from a fresh account fit; every one after it did not, and the refusal is indistinguishable from a rejected theme, so it too surfaced as "check your connection". The checks now avoid re-proving the same document twice — each part of a submission validates itself and only cross-checks the links between them — which leaves every guarantee in place: the daily limit of three, the fixed author name, and the rule that a submission and its quota can only be written together.
- **A theme saved before this update could no longer be submitted to the community gallery.** Submission requires a complete settings snapshot with exactly today's set of appearance keys, and a theme created before this release's 76 new panel choices arrived was missing all of them on disk — Submit reloads and re-validates from the saved file, so it hit that check immediately and failed with a generic "cannot be submitted" message that gave no way to recover. Choosing **Submit to community** now backfills a theme to the current schema first, from the same per-face defaults a brand-new theme of that layout would get, before handing it to the submission screen.
- **Chat's title bubble now honours the Text tab.** Its title was drawn as a bare line rather than through the shared title helper every other face uses, so weight, tracking and the case control just added to this line all had no effect there — only the family, colour and size were ever read. The bubble keeps its own bold, single-line, start-aligned shape; every control now reaches it.
- **Control style no longer sits on the Player page doing nothing on most layouts.** The picker only ever changes a face's own icon glyphs (their opacity and size), and only ten of the twenty faces draw any: Classic and the icon-transport curated faces — Vinyl, Poster, Studio, Halo, Aurora, Eclipse, Spectrum, Material and Expressive — plus Frame and Ribbon, whose tap-confirmation flash still honours it even though neither keeps a permanent icon on screen. On the other eight — Immersive, Depth, Carousel, Chat, Split, Note, Verse and Metadata — moving through Default, Minimal, Compact and Contrast changed nothing at all, because none of them draws an icon the setting can reach. It now appears only on the twelve faces where it does something.
- **Title text behaviour finally applies to the preview on every face.** The setting decides what a title too long for the screen does — shrink, wrap to two, three or five lines, scroll, or simply cut off — and on the wrist it has always reached all eighteen faces. In the phone's preview it reached three: Classic, Poster and Studio. Everywhere else the miniature drew its own fixed behaviour and ignored the setting completely, so on Expressive, Carousel, Split, Verse, Spectrum, Depth, Immersive, Vinyl, Halo, Aurora, Eclipse and Material you could move the control through all six values and watch nothing happen — the one place you go to see what a title will look like was the one place that would not show you. All of them now follow it. Two details came out of matching the wrist properly rather than approximately: the wrap modes keep the size the face designed and cut the last line off, where the preview used to quietly shrink the text to make it fit, and **Cut off after one line** now actually ends with an ellipsis instead of letting a long title run past the edge of the face. Faces whose title is a fixed single line on the watch too — Chat's message bubble, Metadata's table header — are deliberately left alone, because there the miniature was already right. Note's one sentence is the exception and is covered separately below.
- **"Adapt colour for contrast" stopped darkening text onto a black screen.** With **Adapt clock colour for contrast** on (and its title and artist twins), the colour is pushed away from whatever is behind it so it stays readable — except that "whatever is behind it" was always read off the album cover, even on the layouts that put no cover on screen at all. Frame, Ribbon, Note, Verse and Metadata all default to a plain black backdrop, so a bright album was measured, judged too light, and the text was *darkened* — onto black. The Frame clock was the visible case: a dark maroon time on a black dial, from a switch whose whole job is to prevent exactly that. The measurement now asks what the screen is actually showing, so a background that hides the artwork is read as the black field it paints and the text is lifted instead. The same correction reaches the **Dynamic** clock colour, which was picking black text for the same reason. Over a real cover nothing changes: it is still measured where the line sits, and still only an estimate, since the dim scrim and the authored backgrounds darken the picture unevenly.
- **Title text behaviour now works on the Note face.** Note draws the track as one *Artist: Title* sentence, and that sentence is its title — but it was the single face that never asked what the setting said. It wrapped to at most three lines and cut off whatever was left, no matter which of the six behaviours was chosen, so scrolling, shrinking and **Cut off after one line** all did nothing there. It now follows the setting like every other face, on the wrist and in the phone's preview, which drew its own fixed line for the same reason and now plans the sentence exactly as the watch lays it out — including the cover disc, which shifts up as the sentence grows instead of letting it run towards the elapsed time. Three lines remains the ceiling the composition has room for, so **Wrap to 5 lines** stops there. The title's slant, tracking and opacity reach that sentence now too: they were being dropped at the same spot the artist's controls were before 3.3.
- **A long title no longer draws over the artist in the preview.** Once titles could wrap, the second line had to go somewhere. On the curated faces it pushes the artist down, on Depth and Immersive — which stack upward from the bottom edge — it grows towards the top, and on Carousel it re-measures the width, because the usable space on a round screen narrows the further down the screen you reach. This is what the faces themselves do on the watch; the preview simply had no wrapped titles to place before now.
- **The always-on preview stopped showing every face the same way.** Two mistakes, both quiet. Every curated layout drew its always-on title at Poster's larger size, so eleven faces were previewed a size too big; only Poster uses it. And the layouts that centre their always-on text — Poster, Studio, Eclipse, Immersive and Depth — are allowed two lines on the watch but were cut to one here, which made a long title look far more truncated in the preview than it is on the wrist.
- **The title behaviour setting works on the Classic face.** Picking "Cut off after one line", "Wrap to 3 lines" or "Wrap to 5 lines" did nothing there — the face only understood plain "Wrap" and quietly treated the other three as "Smart". Worse, the setting was being undone moments after it was applied: the code that re-applies the title's size on every typography change reset the behaviour to Smart at the same time, so *none* of the six choices had any effect on Classic, including scrolling and shrinking. All six work now, and the table that decides how many lines each wrapping mode gets is shared by the three renderers that need it instead of each keeping its own copy.
- **The Chat face preview shows the layout the watch actually draws.** The face stopped showing past tracks as incoming bubbles a while ago — on a 192dp screen they came straight out of the two elements that matter, and the queue button is what looking further back is for — and replaced them with the current track named in its own outgoing bubble above the voice note. The preview was still drawing the old version.
- **Each curated face previews its own artist styling.** Vinyl, Poster, Studio, Halo, Eclipse and Material each set that line differently — Eclipse's is a widely spaced whisper, Vinyl's a tight line of capitals — and the preview drew one flat treatment for all of them, so the detail these layouts are told apart by was exactly the detail that went missing. Verse's artist line was drawn fully opaque where the face dims it.
- **The Metadata face previews all six of its content blocks.** Credits and Identifiers had no sample rows at all, so those two switches were the only ones on the page that changed nothing — on the face whose whole argument is how much detail fits. Both now show a row, with Identifiers appearing only once turned on, as on the watch. The preview also stopped cutting the table at a flat five rows: it now spends the same budget the watch does, counted per *line* rather than per row, and skips a row that will not fit instead of ending the table there. ## 3.2

### Added

- **Synced lyrics on the watch.** A new screen that follows the song line by line: the line before, the line being sung and the line next, with the current one picked out in your theme colour and a thin bar under it filling as that line runs out — a lyric can hold for the better part of ten seconds, and it is the one thing a wall of words cannot tell you. It holds the display on for as long as it is open, since reading along involves touching nothing and the watch's normal timeout would blank it mid-verse; lowering your wrist still dims the watch — that gesture belongs to Wear OS and no app can override it — so the screen has its own dimmed version that keeps the current line visible and catches up when you raise your wrist again. Put it on any button, gesture, mini button or menu slot — it appears in the action picker as "Lyrics". The words are looked up by the **phone**, not the watch: a watch paired over Bluetooth has no internet of its own, so doing it the other way round would have worked on the LTE models and quietly failed on every other one. They come from [LRCLIB](https://lrclib.net), a free public database that matches on the track name, artist and length alone — no account, no API key, and nothing tied to whichever app is playing, so it works the same whether that's Spotify, YouTube Music or a file on your phone. Lyrics are held only while the app is running and are never written to disk. The file's own `[offset]` correction is honoured — LRC carries one when whoever timed the words was working from a different master or a release with different leading silence, and ignoring it leaves that one track wrong by a fixed amount. Files carrying per-word karaoke timing are shown as ordinary lines rather than as raw markup. If the track has words but nobody has timed them, they show as a plain block you can scroll with the crown or your finger; if the lookup fails, it says so rather than claiming the song has no lyrics — those are different things and only one of them is worth retrying. Music-note characters in the words (`♩ ♪ ♫ ♬ 🎵 🎶 🎼`, which lyric files use constantly to mark instrumental passages) are drawn as the Svartifoss mark, in the colour of the line they sit in. Tapping a line jumps the song to it — the way back to a verse you want to hear again, or the start of one you just missed. It lands exactly on the line you tapped, so that line becomes the current one immediately rather than the one before it, and the watch buzzes to confirm. A paused track stays paused and simply moves to that point. The lookup can be switched off entirely under Settings → Apps → Music apps & services.
- **A new watch face: Metadata.** What the track *is*, rather than what it looks like: a small cover and the title, then as much of the record's real detail as the screen holds — album and your position on it, length, genre, year, who wrote it, and for a track stored on your phone the format, bitrate, sample rate, channels and file size. How many rows fit is measured from your watch rather than fixed, and nothing is said twice — the album artist is dropped when it is just the artist again, and the length is dropped when the track time is already on screen. It **never shows a blank**: a row exists only when the playing app actually published that tag, so a streaming track that gives three details draws three rows rather than twenty labelled dashes — a short table is the honest signal that a player is stingy with its metadata. It also shows **how** the track is reaching you, which is the half a streaming listener can actually see: the elapsed position to the millisecond against the track's full length, the playback speed when it is not the ordinary one, whether the sound is going to the phone's speaker, headphones, Bluetooth or a cast device, whether the audio is a stream or a file on the phone, the server it is streaming from, the file's own name, whether your player has it downloaded, and the track's full address on a line of its own. That block is the one a streaming track can fill when the file details are empty by construction — there is no file to measure, but there is always an address and always an output. Which *kinds* of detail compete for the screen is up to you: album details, release details, credits, playback details, file details and identifiers are six separate switches under Watch appearance, stored per face like every other appearance setting, so turning off the file details is how you make room for the credits. The file details need the same media access the queue covers use and appear only for local tracks; without it, or on streaming, those rows are simply absent.
- **Optional online lookup for track details.** Under Settings → Apps → Music apps & services, **off by default**. It fills in what the playing app did not publish — album, genre, year, record label, release date, country and ISRC — from [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org), a free public database that needs no account and no key. Only the track name and artist are sent, and only if you both switch it on and use the Metadata face. The face never waits for it: your player's own tags are on the wrist immediately and the extra rows appear afterwards if the lookup finds a confident match. It only ever fills a *gap*: whatever your player published wins, because that describes the thing actually coming out of the speaker. That is what makes it worth having for a service like SoundCloud, which publishes almost no tags — every row it adds is one that was blank. It is off by default because a match on a name alone is genuinely ambiguous for live versions and remixes, and a wrong catalogue number does not look wrong.
- **A new watch face: Verse.** The song's words on the main screen, three lines at a time — the one before, the one now, and the one next. The line being sung takes the album's colour, over the same black-and-accent floor the lyrics screen uses, so the two read as one feature on two surfaces. A hairline under the current line fills as *that line* runs out rather than the track — it answers the question the track bar can't ("how long has this line got?") and keeps the screen from sitting perfectly still between one line and the next. The progress arc and the mini-button row are off by default here, since the composition runs edge to edge; both can be switched back on for this face like any other appearance setting. A track with no synced lyric — which is common — gets a title card rather than an error, and lyrics are only looked up while this face is actually selected. It was designed around a serif — a face whose whole subject is written language shouldn't look like a dashboard — so if you want the look it was drawn for, set Lyrics typeface to Marcellus.
- **Song lyrics follow your chosen typeface, and can be given one of their own.** A new **Lyrics typeface** setting under Watch appearance → Text styles the words on the lyrics screen and the Verse face — the same catalogue as everywhere else, no separate list. Left alone it follows the track typeface, exactly as the clock does. Stored **per face**, like the rest of the appearance settings, so a face built around a display type can have one while your everyday face leaves it alone, and a saved theme carries the choice with it.
- **Eight more always-on display styles.** The always-on picker offered five designs while the awake screen offered seventeen faces — and the odd part is that every one of those faces already had an always-on variant written for it. They were only reachable through "Follow face", meaning the only way to have Note's always-on was to use Note awake as well. Poster, Studio, Immersive, Carousel, Chat, Split, Note and Verse can now be picked independently, so the screen you glance at and the screen you use no longer have to be the same design.
- **A new appearance setting: Split panel.** The lower half of the Split face can now be the album artwork, blurred, instead of a flat swatch of one colour taken from it. The cover runs across the whole screen as a single image — sharp above the seam, soft below it — so the panel behind the text is the record you are listening to rather than an average of it. The seam stays a hard edge, which is what keeps the notification-card look the face is built on. Under Watch appearance → Player → Player appearance, and **blurred is now the default**; the original solid colour is still there if you preferred it. Split is the one face the Album background styles cannot reach — it paints its own backdrop by design — so until now there was no way to change that half at all.
- **A new appearance setting: Accent floor.** The curved wash of album colour along the bottom of the screen — the one the lyrics screen and the Verse face use — is now a piece any face can wear, under Watch appearance → Background, in three strengths. It was welded into the face it was designed for, which meant the only way to have it was to accept everything else that face decided too. Off by default everywhere except Verse.
- **Keep the screen on.** A new switch under Watch appearance → Player stops the watch blanking on its inactivity timeout while the player is up, for when you are watching the screen rather than glancing at it. Set **per face**, because whether you want it depends on what you are looking at: a face you read wants it, the one you glance at on the way past should not be spending battery on it. Off by default everywhere. One caveat worth knowing — it defeats the timeout, not the wrist-down gesture, which belongs to Wear OS and no app can override. The lyrics screen ignores the switch and always stays on.
- **The queue opens on the song you are listening to.** It always opened at the top, so on anything longer than a few tracks the one actually playing was somewhere below and finding it was a scrolling job. It now centres on it — on opening, and again whenever the song changes while you have the queue up. How it gets there depends on how far it has to go: a track or two away it scrolls, so you can see which way the list moved; eighty tracks away it repositions outright rather than spending several seconds flying past everything in between. The phone also sends far enough down the queue to reach the track you are on **and what follows it**, instead of always sending the first twenty — being on track eighty used to mean the one you were listening to was not in the list at all, and you had to tap "load more" until it turned up. Asking to load more does not drag you back, and if the playing track cannot be identified at all the list is left where it is rather than jumping to the top. "Load more" itself now shows the same pulsing bars that mark the playing track while it waits, instead of a circular spinner that belonged to no other row in the list.
- **The watch moves to the next track the moment the current one ends.** When the watch already holds the queue, it knows what follows the playing track, so it no longer waits to be told: the title, artist, cover and track time all change as the song runs out rather than a beat or two later. This is a prediction, not a new source of truth — the phone remains the authority, and whatever it says on arrival is applied, correcting the guess if it was wrong. It stays quiet wherever the queue cannot say what is next: with shuffle on (the order the phone published is not necessarily the order that will play), on repeat-one, on the last track the watch has loaded, and when the list showing is play history rather than a real queue — in all of those it simply behaves as it did before.
- **26 more languages.** French, Turkish, Vietnamese, Czech, Swedish, Norwegian Bokmål, Hungarian, Arabic, Hindi, Filipino, Kazakh, Japanese, Korean, Polish, Traditional Chinese, Ukrainian, Thai, Hebrew, Danish, Finnish, Slovak, Bulgarian, Serbian, Croatian, Malay and Bengali join the fourteen the app already shipped in. Plurals follow each language's own grammar rather than English's one/other split — Arabic carries the full six CLDR categories, Polish/Ukrainian/Slovak use one/few/many/other, Hebrew is one/two/other, and languages with no grammatical plural at all (Vietnamese, Turkish, Kazakh, Japanese, Korean, Thai, Malay, and — already shipped — Indonesian and Chinese) correctly use only "other".

### Changed

- **The first launch on the watch teaches the player one screen at a time.** It used to be a single dark sheet listing every gesture at once, in four stacked lines of 12sp text on a screen the size of a coin - and a tap anywhere dismissed it for good, so an exploratory tap while reading skipped the whole thing with no way back. It is now four short pages, one idea each: the centre of the screen, the four edge zones, the swipes, and the crown and side buttons. Back steps to the previous page instead of closing, only the explicit final action marks it as read, and closing it any other way leaves it to be offered again next time the app opens. Each page also says where the matching setting lives on the phone, since every one of these gestures is reassignable and nothing on the watch said so. The guide is versioned, so an expanded one can be shown once to someone who already finished the old single screen.
- **The usage guide on the phone is grouped by where each thing is configured.** Its cards were one long list in gesture order, which is the order you *use* them and not the order you set them up - so a card explaining a swipe and a card explaining the quick actions panel sat next to each other while their settings live on different tabs. The cards now sit under headings for Controls, Actions and watch navigation, and each one names the exact path to the screen that configures it. The tip that used to close the guide now opens it, where it works as a short map of the two screens most of the setup happens on.

- **Settings and Watch appearance keep their familiar swipe navigation with a clearer structure.** The named tabs can be tapped or reached by swiping sideways, while each page now groups options by purpose: auto-start exceptions sit with auto-start, Apps separates lyrics/metadata, streaming, queue covers and Android access, Text contains typography rather than colors, and Panels separates volume, seek, Quick actions, queue and shared effects. The same preferences, per-face values, themes, defaults, backup format and Phone → Watch synchronization remain in place.
- **Settings Search is available from every main tab and understands the new hierarchy.** Results show **tab › section › category**, section names are searchable, and raw placeholders such as `%s` no longer appear as summaries. Flex axes are searchable now. A result whose row depends on another face, font, background or color mode opens the prerequisite choice and explains why instead of landing on an invisible option.
- **Control assignments are readable without decoding their icons.** Swipe and mini-button tiles show both their position and every assigned action; physical-button rows show single, double and long-press assignments. The action picker now has search, a visible Back button and the current subcategory as its title.
- **The Chat face puts your mini buttons in its own circles instead of underneath them.** Chat has always carried a row of round actions under the conversation, and the shared mini-button row sits in that same band — so anyone who turned the row on for this face got two sets of controls drawn on top of one another. Its circles now *are* the mini buttons: one circle per slot you configured, running that slot's own tap and long press, with the row re-fitting itself so a third one still fits inside the screen. Configure nothing and it keeps the queue and skip-next pair it has always had, since the thread occupies the band any other shortcut would live in. What you can still change about them is what belongs to a button rather than to a row: the background style and the group opacity apply exactly as they do everywhere else, and "Follow layout" — the default — is what keeps this face's own album-coloured circle. What you cannot is where they go: this face places them itself, so the curve and shape settings do not reach them, and rather than leaving two settings that quietly do nothing, they are hidden while Chat is selected. Because the collision is gone, Chat no longer starts with the mini-button row switched off — it was only off to keep it from landing on the conversation.

- **App icons on the watch are the app's notification mark, not its launcher icon.** The playing app is already identified on the wrist by the small monochrome glyph it puts in its own notification — on the Split face's seam, and beside the artist line on every other face. The action menu answered the same question ("which app is this row?") with the full-colour launcher icon, so two rows inches apart spoke in two different visual languages. Rows like "Play YouTube Music" now use the same glyph, tinted to whatever colour that screen is using. The glyph is remembered from the last time that app played, which is what makes it work at all: the whole reason to tap "Play YouTube Music" is that YouTube Music is *not* playing, so its notification does not exist at that moment. An app this phone has never seen post a media notification keeps its launcher icon until it plays once — and when it does, every row already assigned to it picks the glyph up on its own, with nothing to re-select. Saved streaming shortcuts are unaffected where they have a fetched cover — that is real artwork, not an app icon.
- **One loading animation everywhere.** The spinning arc shown while the queue, the action menu or a lyric is loading is now the same three pulsing bars that already marked the playing track in the queue and appeared when you tapped "Load more". The arc was a second visual vocabulary — a generic platform shape sitting inside a screen that already had a house animation for exactly this, and one that reads as *audio*. Three bars pulsing say "your music app is working" in a way a rotating arc never does.

### Fixed

- **Settings stop reaching the watch, and it keeps whatever it last received.** The clearest sign was a watch that would not follow the phone: a theme applied on the phone left the previous one on the wrist, a theme created on the phone never appeared in the watch's own face picker, and the language stayed as it was while the phone app was already in another one. All one fault, in the one channel all three travel down. Settings cross to the watch two ways — a durable item that survives the watch sleeping, and an immediate message for when it is awake — and the immediate one was only sent *after* the durable one had been accepted. So a single rejection stopped everything: the durable item is retried unchanged, meaning anything the connection refuses is refused again forever, and the message that would have worked was never reached. What made it refusable is that the durable item carried the phone's **whole** settings file, including your saved streaming shortcuts, your search history and your recently-played list — none of which the watch has ever read — against a fixed size limit shared with the settings that do matter. Enough saved per-face appearance values, which "Apply this look to all faces" creates eighteen at a time, and it simply stopped fitting. The watch now receives only what it actually reads, the immediate message is sent regardless of what happens to the durable one, and the app logs a warning as the payload approaches the limit instead of failing in silence. Your history and saved links stay on the phone where they belong.
- **The watch app follows the language you chose, everywhere and not just on most screens.** The player, queue, menu and face picker already did; the ongoing notification, both Tiles, the album-art complication, the "Nothing playing" and error lines on the player itself, and the button names the watch reports back to the phone's Controls screen did not — none of those is a screen, so none of them was ever told, and they kept using whatever language the watch itself is set to. With a phone in Portuguese and a watch in English that showed up as English text mixed into an otherwise translated app. All of them now resolve the same chosen language, re-read each time so a change still applies without restarting anything.
- **Your own themes come first in the app's Watch themes list too.** They sat under all eighteen built-in faces, so switching between themes you had made meant scrolling past the entire built-in list — the same fix the watch's own picker already got. The heading is left out entirely when you have not made any.
- **A per-face default no longer disagrees with its switch while a custom theme is active.** The Watch settings screen read the plain default for on/off options in a saved theme where the watch read the face's own default, so a switch could show one position while the watch obeyed the other, and the only way to reconcile them was to toggle it — which writes an explicit value and hits the one path that always worked. The text and number options had always consulted it.

- **The Watch preview updates for every registered appearance preference.** Its listener previously carried a partial hand-written copy of the theme registry, so changing clock metrics, Flex axes, several palette controls, list sizing or mini-button mode could leave the miniature stale until the screen was reopened. The preview now observes the same scoped registry themes and backup use; its multi-color and source-icon controls also read the active face rather than a global value.
- **Face-specific settings no longer appear where they do nothing.** Metadata content is visible only on the Metadata face and Split panel only on Split. Settings Search still finds them and leads to the face choice when necessary. Invalid numeric typography values now state the accepted range instead of silently refusing the dialog.
- **The mini buttons look the same on the watch as they do in the preview.** Two of the fifteen background styles were drawn from separate copies of the same rules on the phone and on the watch, and the copies had come apart. "Glow (Expressive)" previewed its hairline and its icon in a colour the watch never used — the watch lifts a dark album colour to something that survives being drawn one pixel wide, and the preview did not. And every solid style picked its icon colour by a rule that put white ink on the lighter Expressive surfaces, where the watch correctly used black. Both sides now read one shared definition, so the preview can no longer promise a button the wrist will not draw.

- **The playing app's own buttons in the quick actions panel are drawn the way that app draws them.** With the panel set to mirror the current media app, YouTube Music's shuffle arrived as two bare strokes and the "on" dot with both arrowheads missing, and its repeat button was silently swapped for this app's own repeat glyph. Two unrelated faults with one appearance. An action icon belongs to the app that published it and is loaded through a context built from that app — and whichever part of it fails to resolve there is not drawn at all, which the flat white tint meant to guard against cannot help with, since a tint only recolours what *did* draw. The pass that centres a glyph then scaled the surviving fragment up until it filled the button, which is why it looked like a deliberate icon rather than a broken one. Each icon is now rendered every way it can legitimately be loaded and the render that drew the most of the glyph is the one that goes to the watch; a button shipped as a state list or a transition is also settled before it is captured, instead of being photographed mid-change. Repeat was being painted over deliberately, so that repeat-one could be shown — but a player publishes its repeat button per state anyway, so its own icon already said which state it was in. This app's glyph is now used there only when the player supplied no icon at all, which is also when it was always the right answer.
- **Those icons are no longer drawn a quarter smaller than the system's.** They crossed to the watch at 48 pixels and were then placed at exactly that size, because the watch fits them in a way that shrinks a large picture but never enlarges a small one — so inside a 52dp button they sat at 24dp, and smaller still on a denser watch, while this app's own glyphs beside them filled their buttons properly. They are now sent large enough for any watch and fitted to the button, at the size the phone's own media controls draw them.
- **"Reset to default" now actually resets.** In the numeric settings — blur radius, opacity, offsets, hue shift — tapping *Reset to default* wrote the default away in the background but left the box showing the old number, so pressing OK immediately afterwards put the old number straight back. The only way to make a reset stick was to tap it, dismiss the dialog, reopen it and press OK. It now applies the default and closes, in one tap, through the same path OK uses — so everything that reacts to the setting, the watch-face preview included, is told about it.
- **"Carousel card shape" no longer appears on layouts that ignore it.** It sat in the Player settings for every face, but only the Carousel layout draws a card silhouette — so on the other seventeen it was a picker that changed nothing, which reads as a broken setting rather than an inapplicable one. It now shows only while Carousel is selected, the way the Expressive face's seek control already did.
- **Changing track updates the watch straight away instead of waiting for you to pause.** Two separate faults, with the same symptom. The phone learns what is playing from the music app's own metadata callback, and it was only listening for that while a session was actively playing — but plenty of players drop out of the playing state for a moment between tracks, which is exactly when the new track is announced, so the announcement went into nothing and the phone caught up only once playback resumed. Any unrelated playback event would also let it catch up, which is why *pausing* appeared to fix a watch still showing the previous song: pausing is such an event. The phone now listens for metadata on idle sessions too. Separately, the playing track was sent to the watch only as a Data Layer item — durable, and what the watch reloads itself from after sleeping, but routed through Play Services' replication layer and slow enough that changing song visibly lagged on the wrist. The same state now also goes out as a message, which arrives immediately. The cover still travels the slower way, since an image legitimately takes longer than a line of text — the title and artist no longer wait for it.
- **A track change while the music is paused no longer drops the watch to "Nothing playing".** The same metadata handler published "the session that is playing", which is nothing at all when playback is paused — so loading a new track without starting it emptied the watch screen instead of showing that track. It now publishes the session the watch is actually being shown.
- **The playback position on the watch no longer drifts away from the song.** Three faults, all of them ending as a progress ring and a track time that were quietly wrong. The phone told the watch *when* it had measured the position, as a timestamp, and the watch subtracted that from its own clock — only correct if the two agree to the millisecond, and Wear OS syncs a watch's time from its phone with tolerance rather than exactly, so however far apart they sat became a fixed error for the whole track. It now sends **how old** the reading is instead: a duration has no clock to disagree about. The trip between the two devices was then unaccounted for entirely, and is now measured — the watch stamps its request, the phone hands the stamp back untouched, and the watch times the round trip itself. And because the phone deliberately stops resending the position while a song plays steadily, nothing ever checked the result: the watch now asks where playback actually is, on a schedule that stretches out to about once a minute while its count is tracking well and tightens straight back up when it is not. Small differences are eased out over a couple of checks so nothing visibly jumps; a large one is corrected outright. Play, pause, skip, seek, changing track and the phone coming back into range each trigger a check of their own.
- **The track time resets when the track changes.** A 2:30 song ending into a 4:00 one left the watch counting 2:31, 2:32 and onwards to 4:00 instead of starting over. A music app publishes its track details and its playback position through two separate channels with no guaranteed order, so for a moment after a change the new track's name and length are readable while the position still belongs to the one that just ended — and the phone was pairing them up and sending that. The phone now recognises a position reading older than the track it is about to be attached to and reports the start of the track instead.
- **Skip and restart move the clock immediately.** Asking for the next track and then waiting to be told where playback is asks a question whose answer is already known. The watch now snaps the position to zero the moment you press skip, previous or restart, rather than carrying on counting through the track you just left until the phone replies. The track name still waits for the phone, because that genuinely is not known on the watch — only the part that is certain moves early.
- **Pausing and resuming no longer jumps the position.** The clock difference above, showing up differently: a paused track displays exactly what the phone measured, while a playing one was displayed through the error — so every pause and every resume stepped by that difference. It moved the progress ring and the track time alike. Tapping play on the watch could also throw the position forward for a frame before it settled.
- **Album art is no longer occasionally stretched.** Two covers of different shapes — an ordinary square one and a wide thumbnail, which is what some apps publish — were cross-faded inside a container that took the larger width and the larger height *separately*, producing a box belonging to neither and squashing both pictures into it. Rare, because it needed two consecutive covers of differing shape, and it corrected itself on the next song, which is why it was hard to catch. Covers of matching shape cross-fade exactly as before; mismatched ones are now fitted individually first.
- **The watch stopped doing invisible work while dimmed.** With the wrist down, the playback position was still being recalculated twice a second — 120 updates a minute, each one redrawing the always-on face — on a screen the system physically refreshes about once in that minute. None of it was ever seen. The position is now refreshed once per always-on update instead, which is the moment the screen actually redraws, so the always-on progress and track time are correct exactly when it matters and left alone in between. The mechanism to do this had been written and simply never switched on.
- **"Up Next" on the always-on display now has data on every face.** The pill was generalised to every always-on design a while back, but the queue behind it was only refreshed for two of them, so the rest showed a stale track or nothing at all.
- **Watch faces stopped overriding your appearance settings.** An audit of all seventeen turned up several places where a face painted something itself instead of asking what you had chosen, so the setting silently did nothing on that face. Chat drew its title and artist in fixed white, so the colour treatments, custom colours and adaptive contrast did nothing there. Vinyl, Halo, Eclipse and Aurora did the same to the artist line. Chat's waveform is a progress indicator and now takes the progress colour like every other one. Each of these now asks, and each still keeps its own designed opacity, so nothing changes for anyone who never set the preference in the first place.
- **The watch's face picker puts the ones you actually use at the top.** Recently used first, most recent leading — kept separately for your own themes and for the built-in faces, so a saved theme never lands in the middle of the built-in list. Faces you have never picked keep the order the collection was designed in rather than being reshuffled. The order is remembered on the watch and never sent to the phone.
- **Your own themes come first in the watch's face picker.** They sat below all seventeen built-in faces, so switching between themes you had made meant scrolling past the whole built-in list every time.

### Fixed

- **The watch clock no longer shows the previous song's colour.** With the clock set to follow the album colour, it kept the accent of the track *before* the one playing, and only caught up when some unrelated setting happened to refresh it. The colour of a cover is worked out in the background, and the clock was being repainted immediately — before that work finished — so it read whatever the last cover had left behind. It is now repainted when the new colour actually lands. The always-on display was already doing this correctly, and nothing else was affected: the title, artist, progress, mini buttons and background are all resolved at the same moment the colour arrives.
- **App icons in the watch's action rows are no longer album-cover sized.** Rows like "Play YT Music" drew the app's icon as large as real album artwork and cropped it into a circle, so a brand logo was the biggest thing on the row — larger than the actual covers beside it — with the corners of its squircle sliced off. Only genuine cover art (a streaming shortcut's fetched thumbnail) fills the circle now; an app icon sits at a modest size, keeping its own colours and shape, and monochrome icons are unchanged. This affected both the actions menu and the quick actions panel, which had the same rule written twice.

- **The colour harmonies now actually look different on the watch.** Triadic, Complementary and Analogous each showed three distinct swatches when you picked them, then rendered identically to Expressive on the watch. The reason: they only ever rotated the two supporting colours and left the main one on the album's own shade — and most of the watch paints the main colour and nothing else. The Note face paints exactly one colour, so on it the five harmonies were provably identical. Each of those three now turns the whole palette by the angle that names it, so the accent itself changes with the treatment while the harmony's internal relationships stay intact. Monochrome and Duotone still don't rotate, on purpose: Monochrome is the fallback for covers with no usable colour, and Duotone's two shades are real colours out of the artwork, which rotating would replace with invented ones. Near-greyscale covers are unaffected throughout — turning a grey produces another grey, so those still get a light-to-dark ladder rather than a fake tint.
- **The Note face shows the palette's second colour.** It draws one line and nothing else, so with the title fixed at white the only colour on screen was the accent. The title now takes the palette's supporting shade whenever that shade is far enough from the artist's colour to read as a genuinely different one — which covers the harmonies and also Expressive, whose supporting slots are separate colours out of the cover. Where the palette has no real second colour (Normal, Desaturated, Monochrome, and any near-greyscale cover) the title stays white, as before. The shade is lifted to the same brightness floor every other album-derived text on the watch uses, so a dark album can't leave the title one shade off invisible. Its miniature on the phone also honours your chosen title colour and opacity, which it had been the one face to ignore.
- **The "Up Next" cover in the quick actions panel is the right size, and properly round.** Every other row in the panel grows its cover to fill the pill; the Up Next row kept a fixed small thumbnail, so the one row showing the actual next track had the smallest artwork on screen. That row also carries extra padding above and below for its two lines of text, which left the cover less height than it had been sized for — so it came out an oval with the top and bottom of the artwork sliced off, while every other row stayed circular. The pill now gives a cover the same room the other rows do, and the cover can never ask for more height than the pill has.
- **The Split face's app icon no longer sits on a coloured disc.** The icon is drawn on its own now, which is what the seam between cover and panel wanted; it takes the space the disc used to occupy, and is shaded against the panel so it stays visible.
- **The playing app's icon no longer blinks out on every track change.** When a track changes, the phone sends the new title and artist immediately and the cover a moment later, so the text doesn't have to wait for the artwork to cross Bluetooth. The album art knows to hold the previous image during that gap; the app icon didn't, so it vanished and reappeared each time. It now holds too.
- **The playing app's icon is no longer pixelated.** It was being sent at 48 pixels square, which was fine when it was drawn beside the artist line but not on the Split face, which shows it three times that size. It is now sent at a resolution that holds up at the largest size any face draws it. The quick panel's action icons are unchanged — those are drawn small, and there are up to five of them per update.
- **The watch face preview shows a real app icon.** Where the preview stands in for the playing app's icon it drew a small shape assembled from a circle and a rectangle, meant to read as a music note. It now uses Svartifoss's own launcher icon, so the miniature shows an actual app icon at the size and weight a real one will have.
- **Face-specific defaults now actually reach the watch.** A default that a face sets for itself — the edge progress arc being off on the new faces, for instance — was honoured by the settings screen but ignored by the watch for any on/off setting. The switch read "off" while the watch kept drawing the element, and it only came right after toggling it on and then off again, because that finally wrote an explicit value. Both sides now resolve these the same way.
- **The watch no longer crashes on a track change with the album-art fade on.** On watches running Android 11 and older, the blurred background is built in software, and the previous blur was being freed the instant the new one was set — while the cross-fade was still drawing it. Every faded track change on those watches risked "trying to use a recycled bitmap". The old image is now simply released to the garbage collector, which has managed bitmap memory since Android 8.
- **The watch no longer fails to open with "Multiple observers registered".** Phone notifications were delivered over a process-wide bus that allowed exactly one listener, while the listener was the now-playing screen — so any overlap of two instances of that screen killed the app as it started. The "show each notification once" behaviour now belongs to the screen instead of the bus.
- **Far fewer Tile refresh requests.** The watch asked the system to refresh its Tile and complication on every music update, which the phone sends about once a second while a track plays. It now asks only when something the Tile actually shows has changed. (This also makes a crash inside the Wear Tiles library itself much rarer; that one is not ours to fix.)

- **Tapping a track in the watch queue plays it again.** Three separate things could swallow the tap. Some music apps run more than one media session, and the queue you see can come from a different one than the app plays through — the jump now goes to the session that published the entries as well as the playing one. Closing the queue in the same tap could cancel the message before it was ever sent. And the jump was being withheld from players that don't *declare* support for it, even though many implement it perfectly well; it is now always attempted, and only if playback demonstrably doesn't move does Svartifoss fall back to starting the track by its own id (which is what players like Retro Music, that genuinely don't implement queue jumping, need).
- **Queue covers with black bars are cropped on the watch too.** Some tracks — YouTube Music "art track" thumbnails in particular — wrap the real square cover in flat bars, which left the artwork as a small square floating inside a rectangle. The phone already trimmed the covers it fetched itself, but a cover could reach the watch from other sources untouched; the watch now trims every cover it draws, whichever one sent it.
- **The now-playing bars in the queue never stop animating.** The equalizer on the playing row was frozen for the whole duration of a scroll and sometimes stayed frozen afterwards. The freeze dated from when each bar was laid out individually every frame, which really did cost the scroll its smoothness; the bars are now drawn as one shape that only repaints, cheap enough to simply keep running. Only the scrolling song titles still pause while you move the list, since those do re-lay the whole list out.
- **The idle-screen settings no longer appear on every Settings page.** The "Idle screen" group was only ever meant to show under Automation, but it was left out of the list that hides groups, so it was never hidden at all and turned up on General, Behavior, Apps and Data & support as well.
- **"Force stop" no longer leaves its notification behind.** Force stop ends the app by killing its process outright, which is not an orderly shutdown — the "Music control active" notification was regularly left sitting in the shade afterwards, with buttons that did nothing because there was no longer anything behind them. It is now taken down explicitly before the app goes, and the notification-listener connection is released immediately rather than through a message the dying process might never deliver. The ordinary "Stop" path got the same guarantee.

- **A shortcut no longer opens the music app on your phone before trying to play in the background.** Picking a shortcut on the watch did two things at the same time: it asked the phone to play the link, and — independently — it told the phone to open it. So the app came to the foreground on your phone regardless, even when the background route was about to work, which is exactly what that route exists to avoid when the phone is locked in a pocket. The watch now only sends the request, and the phone reports back: if it managed to start playback, nothing is opened at all. Opening the app is now the last resort it was meant to be, and happens only after every silent route has been tried and failed.
- **A saved playlist that the app refuses to play by address is now played by name.** When the phone asks a music app to play a link in the background, the only command it ever sent for a playlist was the address itself. SoundCloud accepts a track address and ignores a playlist one, so for a saved playlist there was literally no other command left to try, and it fell through to opening the page. The playlist's saved name is now offered as a search afterwards — the same command that already plays an artist, and the one that music apps implement most reliably from outside. The address still goes first, so an app that honours it plays exactly what you asked for and the search never runs.

- **A shortcut to a playlist now plays it, instead of just opening it.** Pasting a playlist link, or using the Likes shortcut, took you to the right page and then left it sitting there — while a link to a single track played fine. Two separate things caused it. Svartifoss asks the music app to play the address directly, and treats the app *accepting* that request as success; SoundCloud accepts a track address happily and quietly ignores a playlist one, so Svartifoss believed it had worked and never tried anything else. It now checks whether playback actually started, and carries on to the other routes when it didn't. And the last of those routes — open the page, then prod the app — was re-sending that same ignored address rather than simply pressing play. It now presses play, but only where that is unambiguous: when the app had nothing playing beforehand, or when it has since loaded something new. An app still sitting on the track it was already playing is left alone, so this can never hijack what you were listening to.
- **The now-playing cover is no longer blurry with apps that publish a small one.** Some streaming apps hand over a thumbnail sized for their own phone notification — SoundCloud's is 100 pixels square — which the watch then stretches across its whole screen. The full-size cover's address is published alongside it, so when it is available, and only when the supplied image is smaller than the watch needs, the bigger copy is fetched and cached instead. This uses the existing **Fetch queue covers online** setting, on by default; with it off the small image is used exactly as before.
- **Search, library browsing and background playback now work with apps built on the current media library.** Svartifoss looked for a music app's browsable library using only the original Android action name. Apps built on Media3 — the current standard, and what SoundCloud ships — are under no obligation to publish that older name, and increasingly don't. The lookup came back empty for those apps, and because that single lookup gates four separate features, Svartifoss concluded the app had no library at all: searching from the watch fell back to cruder routes, "Browse library" reported that the app exposes nothing, and both routes that start playback with the screen off were unavailable. All four now work for apps that publish only the newer name. Apps that publish the old one are unaffected — that name is still tried first and resolves exactly as before.

- **The watch queue no longer passes recently-played tracks off as the upcoming queue.** When the playing app publishes no queue at all — SoundCloud is one — the phone falls back to the list of tracks you recently played, and the watch rendered that fallback exactly like a real queue: same screen, same rows, no label. So the queue looked broken in three separate ways at once. The songs were wrong (they were the ones already played, not the ones coming up), none of them had cover art (a remembered track is only artist and title text, with no artwork to send), and tapping one didn't jump the queue but searched for the track again, which SoundCloud ignores. The list now says "Recently played" above the rows, and names the reason — the playing app doesn't share its queue — so it reads as a different list rather than as a failed one. Nothing changes for apps that do publish a queue.

- **The watch's Like button now shows whether a track is already liked, on apps that only expose "like" as a notification action.** The highlighted-pill state was being read exclusively from the MediaSession custom action that most players (YouTube Music, Retro Music, Spotify) use for their like/favourite button — but SoundCloud, and any app like it, publishes "like" only as a notification action, which that check never looked at. Tapping still toggled it correctly, since a separate path already reached that button to fire it; only the on-screen state was missing, so telling whether a track was already liked meant checking the phone. The watch button now falls back to a best-effort read of that notification action's own label (the same "does it read like 'unlike'/'remove'/'saved' rather than 'like'" heuristic the custom-action path already used) whenever no custom action exists at all, so it stays out of the way for apps the original check already handled correctly.

## 3.1-beta2

**Beta — not a finished release.** Browse your music library from the watch, a "Nothing playing" screen that can actually start something, per-element control over the clock and title text, and a batch of fixes to settings that were shared between faces or unreachable outside English.

### Fixed

- **Beta builds were never offered a newer beta.** The update check compared only the numeric part of a version, so `3.1-beta1` and `3.1-beta2` counted as the same release — the Updates screen fetched the newer beta, showed its release notes, and still reported "You're up to date". The same fault would have withheld the finished 3.1 release from everyone testing a 3.1 beta. Betas are now ordered properly (`3.1-beta1` → `3.1-beta2` → `3.1`), including `beta10` after `beta9`. If you are on 3.1-beta1, this fix cannot reach you through the app — the faulty check is in the build you are running, so install this one manually.
- **Fixed a crash that could kill the app in the background.** A watch command or the notification listener reconnecting (typically at boot) could start the phone service without it ever promoting itself to the foreground in time, and Android killed the process for it. Only the service's creation answered that contract; a start aimed at an already-running service answered nothing.
- **Per-face appearance defaults were ignored for on/off settings.** A face could ship with, say, its edge progress arc off by default and the switch would still read as on — the on/off path skipped per-face defaults entirely, on both the watch and the settings screen, so the two also disagreed with each other. Only newly added defaults were affected; nothing you had set yourself changes.
- **The app no longer disappears from the watch face when you pause.** Pausing and letting the screen turn off used to tear down the watch service half a minute later, taking the ongoing-activity chip and the watch's own media session with it — so coming back meant reopening the app from the launcher. A paused track now holds both for 30 minutes by default, which is what makes "raise your wrist and press play again" work; the duration is configurable.
- **Voice search reaches players it used to miss.** When a library search comes back empty, the "play the best match" command now goes through the same route that wakes a stopped player, instead of at whichever session happened to be playing — which for some apps is not the one that implements it at all. The command is also skipped when the player explicitly says it doesn't support it, rather than fired blindly.
- **Searching from the watch actually plays now.** Picking a search result used to send a play command to the currently tracked session — which does nothing when no session is live yet, the usual state when you start a search from the wrist, and which several apps ignore even when one is. Results now go through the same route that wakes a stopped player.
- **Artists found by search open instead of failing silently.** An artist or album row is a *folder*, not a track, so asking to play it was never going to do anything. Those rows now walk into their contents, so you can search an artist and pick an album from the watch.
- **Seeking from the edge no longer changes the volume instead.** On watches with a touch bezel (every non-Classic Galaxy Watch), sliding a finger around the rim is also reported as rotary input, so an edge-seek drag popped the volume overlay open and the seek never happened. Rotary input is now ignored while a drag on the progress ring is in progress.
- **Carousel: long track titles now wrap instead of being cut off at both ends.** The title sat in a fixed-width block low on the screen, where a round display has barely any width left — so a second line ran straight into the bezel and the face could only ever ellipsize. The block now measures the actual curve at the depth the text reaches, and widens or narrows to match: short titles keep the full width they had, long ones wrap and stay inside the glass. The cover rail also sits very slightly higher to leave that second line somewhere to go.
- **Carousel: the artist line now obeys the Text settings.** Title and artist were styled by different rules on this face — the artist line ignored its size, weight, opacity and letter-spacing settings entirely, so changing them appeared to move only the title. Both lines now go through the same styling as every other face.
- **Carousel: the text no longer drifts away from the cover.** The artist and title positions were separate hand-tuned numbers that happened to line up with the artwork; they are now derived from the cover's own edges, so they stay attached to it.
- **The clock font in the preview matches the watch.** Five of the bundled fonts (Bebas Neue, Playfair Display, Space Grotesk, Orbitron, Caveat) drew the phone preview's clock in the default typeface while the watch rendered them correctly.
- **The phone preview now draws Carousel properly.** Picking Carousel showed a generic curated-face miniature — no cover rail, no neighbours, text in the wrong place — so the preview was of no use for the one face it differs from most. It now renders the actual rail, its card shape, and the same title wrapping the watch does.
- **Carousel: the artist text color setting now does something.** This face read the raw face accent instead of the artist color slot, so the Artist text color treatment (and its custom color) had no effect on it at all.
- **The music app's icon is back next to the artist on Carousel.** This face drew its own copy of the icon instead of using the shared one, and center-cropped it into a circle — but the icon the phone sends is usually the notification's small glyph, which sits on a padded canvas. Cropping a circle out of that padding threw most of the glyph away and left the rest as untinted white, so it read as missing. It now goes through the same renderer every other face uses, which also means the icon size and opacity settings finally apply to it.
- **The mini-button row's position followed you between faces.** Every other mini-button setting — background, curve, opacity, shape — is remembered per face, but the row's vertical offset was shared, so nudging it up to clear one face's controls moved it on all of them. It is now per face like the rest of its group. Your current position is kept until you set one for a face.
- **The clock could freeze at the time it appeared.** "Always show time" is a per-face setting, but the ticker that refreshes the clock every minute read the old shared value — so on a face where the clock was switched on, it could stop updating a minute later and sit there showing a stale time.
- **The phone preview ignored a face's own defaults for on/off settings.** Carousel ships with the edge ring and edge seeking off, and the watch honored that, but the preview drew them on — it only applied per-face defaults to text settings, not to switches and numbers.
- **Half the color treatments were impossible to select in every language but English.** Complementary, Triadic, Analogous, Monochrome and Duotone — the headline feature of 3.1-beta1 — were never added to the translated option lists, and those lists are matched to their values by position. In all twelve translated languages the picker simply stopped at Expressive and the five harmonies could not be chosen at all. The same gap hid the Depth and Carousel faces.
- **Album background styles were labelled wrong in every language but English.** "Frosted edges" was missing from the middle of the translated list rather than the end, so every style after it carried the previous one's name — picking "Square (sharp corners)" actually applied Frosted edges, and so on through 21 styles.
- **Clock settings no longer produce duplicate groups.** Typeface, weight, italic, size and spacing have one Clock group under Text; color, opacity and adaptive contrast have one Clock colors group under Colors. Each concept has one searchable home.
- **Text and color controls have consistent homes.** Title, artist and clock typography stays under Text, while their color policies sit together under Colors with the album palette and surface colors.
- **The clock's type settings no longer appear twice.** Weight, italic, size and letter spacing for the clock showed up on the Player page as well as on Text, because the new group was missing from the list that hides categories outside their own section.
- **The progress ring no longer starts with a mark in the wrong color.** With a multi-hue color treatment the rounded tip at the very start of the bar was painted in the color from the *end* of the ring, leaving a stray dash of the wrong hue at 12 o'clock. The comet ring had the same fault in reverse — its tail, which is meant to fade in from nothing, began with a solid dot.
- **List covers are no longer a small thumbnail floating in a big pill.** The cover was pinned at one fixed size no matter how tall the row was, so the row's padding read as a thick border around it — and picking a larger list row size grew the border instead of the artwork. The cover now fills the row, leaving an even margin on all sides, and is inset from the left by the same amount instead of being pushed in by a text keyline it doesn't need. On the default row size it is about half again as large; on the tall sizes, roughly double. Each style's cover shape is preserved at every size — round styles stay round rather than turning into rounded squares as the cover grows. Applies to the queue, the action menu and the quick panel's full-width rows, which all draw the same pill and each used to pin the cover independently. Monochrome action icons are deliberately left at their old size: those are glyphs, not artwork, and scaling one to fill the pill reads as a fault rather than as a bigger icon.
- **"Use font everywhere" now covers the quick-actions shortcuts.** The panel's own labels followed the chosen font while its shortcut rows stayed on the default typeface, because those rows are built as the panel opens rather than living in the layout.
- **Panel backdrops that were missing in every language but English.** The panel background list stopped at Prism outside English, hiding the newer options entirely.

## 3.1-beta1

**Beta — not a finished release.** Colour treatments that build a real palette from the album instead of repeating one swatch, per-element typography (including a full Google Sans Flex variable-font axis editor) for the track title and artist, and a queue that finally shows covers from local and streaming players alike.

### Watch colours

- **Five new colour treatments.** Alongside Normal / Desaturated / Expressive, the Watch face tab now offers **Complementary** (the album accent plus its opposite hue), **Triadic** (three hues evenly spaced around the wheel), **Analogous** (neighbouring hues, the subtlest of the set), **Monochrome** (one hue in three tones, Material-You style) and **Duotone** (two colours that genuinely appear in the artwork). They apply face-wide or per surface (artist, progress, volume, quick panel) exactly like the existing treatments, and the picker's swatches preview each one's real colours.
- **New: Tone.** A filter applied on top of whichever treatment is selected — **Vibrant**, **Pastel**, **Warm** or **Cool** — so combinations like "triadic but pastel" need no extra treatment of their own. Defaults to None, which changes nothing.
- **New: Color shift.** Every treatment anchors its *main* colour to the album's own hue and only rotates the companion colours, so the main accent stayed the same no matter which treatment you picked. This turns the whole palette around the colour wheel (0–359°), varying that main colour while keeping each harmony's internal angles intact. 0 keeps the album colour as-is; a fixed Normal colour you picked by hand is never shifted.
- **Black-and-white covers no longer get an invented colour.** Every harmony needs a hue to rotate, and a greyscale cover has none; rather than producing three identical greys (or clamping a grey up into an arbitrary tint), the harmonies fall back to a tonal ladder on such artwork.
- The watch, the phone preview and the treatment picker's swatches now derive palettes through one shared implementation, so a colour shown on the phone is the colour the watch renders.

### Typography

- **New "Text" tab**, split out of Player so the font, title, artist and app-icon controls are no longer buried in one long list — each is now its own labelled group.
- **The title and artist can now be styled independently**, on top of the existing font choice: **weight** (a free 1–1000 value, not a fixed dropdown), **italic**, **size** (70–140% of the face's own size), **opacity** and **letter spacing**. Each face's designed look is the default, so nothing changes until you move a control — and a face that deliberately draws its title bold stays bold unless you pick a different weight.
- **New typeface: Google Sans Flex**, a real variable font. Selecting it unlocks four additional sliders — **width**, **optical size**, **grade** and **roundness** — read straight from the font's own axis ranges, so the whole watch face's character is yours to tune, not just its weight.
- **The playing app's icon is now adjustable too** — size and opacity, independently of the artist text next to it.
- All of these are per watch face and travel with custom themes and config backups, like every other appearance setting.

### Playback queue

- **Queue covers now work with local music players.** Players like Retro Music publish each queue entry's cover as a reference into your music library rather than as an image. Reading those needs media access, which the app could no longer hold on Android 13 and newer — so the queue silently showed blank thumbnails. There is now a "Queue covers from your library" row under Settings → Apps that requests it, in context and only when you ask. Nothing is sent anywhere; the queue simply keeps showing blank thumbnails if you decline.
- **Fixed: pausing sent the watch to the "Nothing playing" screen.** A paused track is meant to keep its title with a "Playback Stopped" line; instead the watch dropped to the idle screen. The first update after a pause was correct, so this only struck on the *next* one — a volume change, another app's session appearing, the app reconnecting — which is why it looked intermittent.
- **Queue covers are sent as JPEG rather than lossless PNG.** Cover art is photographic and has no transparency, so PNG was several times larger for no visible gain — the same reasoning the now-playing cover already used. With up to 20 thumbnails in one transfer the difference is large enough that, at the size a Cover queue style asks for, the covers could take so long over Bluetooth that they appeared never to arrive.
- **The playing track's cover now always shows in the queue.** It reuses the cover already on the now-playing screen, so it needs no download and no permission — previously a streaming app could show art on the player while its queue, including the row that was playing, stayed blank.
- **Queue covers now work with streaming players.** Clients like Echo Music and other YouTube Music front-ends publish each queue cover as a link rather than an image, and those were previously skipped outright so that opening the queue could never wait on the network — which left those queues permanently blank. They are now downloaded once each and cached on the phone. **On by default**, because unlike a shortcut thumbnail (which falls back to an app icon) a streaming queue has no other cover source at all; one switch turns it off, in **both** Settings → Apps and the Watch face tab's Panels section, and only the cover URL the player itself published is ever requested.
- **Streaming covers are no longer pixelated.** Those players publish a *list-sized* thumbnail — often 60px, sized for the phone's own compact rows — which looked coarse stretched across the watch's Cover pill. The size is now requested up front, so the cover arrives at the resolution the watch actually draws it at.
- **A slow cover can no longer hold up the queue.** The list is only sent once every entry's cover has resolved, so with the online fetch on that meant waiting on real network requests; each one is now capped, and an entry that doesn't answer in time simply arrives without a cover.
- **Queue covers are found in more places.** Beyond the entry's own image and local URI, the app now also reads the artwork keys apps put in the entry's extras, and can resolve a library track's cover directly.
- **A queue on a second session is no longer missed.** Some players run more than one media session at once — Retro Music ships a separate Wear browser session alongside its playback one — and only one of them carries the queue. If the session that is playing has none, the app now checks that app's other live sessions before falling back to play history.

- **Control style no longer changes the seek backdrop**: the seek overlay's background followed the selected control style, unlike the volume and quick-actions surfaces. It now behaves like them, independent of the control style.
- **Preview blur matches the watch**: the phone preview's background blur was far weaker than the watch's at the same radius; the slider now previews the same strength you get on the watch.

- **Fixed a watch state replay bug.** While the watch was disconnected or asleep, Google Play Services could queue up several updates to the same state (skipping tracks repeatedly, or hopping through themes/settings on the phone) and replay all of them on reconnect; the watch marched through every queued update one by one instead of jumping straight to the latest — visible as playback "catching up" through each intermediate track, or the watch face flickering through each intermediate theme, once you touched it. - **Expressive's clock color now defaults to White**, matching its sibling album-accent faces, instead of Dynamic.

### Bug fixes

- **Legacy drawer queue could still appear**: the guard only checked for an `activeQueueItemId`, which many apps never set on Android 10+, letting the old drawer queue slip through instead of the new `QueueActivity`. Now also blocked by list type (PLAYLIST/HISTORY).
- **Watch round-screen clipping in light mode**: the now-playing clock circle was cut off at the bezel edge; background drawable and button-config vertical spacing corrected.
- **Queue screen stuttered while scrolling**: each row's rounded-corner clip forced an offscreen `saveLayer` per row on every scroll frame; switched to drawing the rounded background directly. The now-playing equalizer and marquee title animations are also frozen while the list is actively scrolling, and the queue header no longer recomposes on every per-second position tick.
- **Current track name missing from the Wear OS recents card**: the app's task label wasn't being updated, so the app switcher only ever showed "Svartifoss" instead of the playing track.
- **Watch-face complication cover flashing back to the placeholder icon**: a complication refresh could land before the phone's album-art asset finished transferring over the Data Layer; the last successfully rendered cover is now cached and reused in that case instead of regressing to the placeholder.
- **Equalizer icon inconsistent across surfaces**: the static notification/ ambient equalizer glyph used different bar geometry than the animated "Up Next" icon and the queue's now-playing indicator; redrawn to match.
- **Saved button configs silently failing to load in release builds**: the proguard keep rule protecting `PhoneAction`'s reflection-based deserialization constructor still targeted the pre-rename package (`com.matejdro.wearmusiccenter`), so R8 shrinking stripped that constructor from every action class in release builds - any saved button config (including a fresh install's defaults) silently loaded empty. Fixed the rule to the current package.
- **Watch UI freezing on track changes (including inside the queue screen)**: several pieces of heavy work ran on the watch's main thread every time the phone pushed new music state - decoding the album-art bitmap (and custom list icons), the watch-face complication re-encoding its cover cache as a max-quality PNG, and the media session re-sending the full cover bitmap across binder on every state update (every volume step and seek included). All bitmap decode/encode now runs on background threads, an unchanged cover is recognized by its Data Layer asset id and skipped outright, and the media-session metadata is only re-sent when something in it changed. A track change also no longer runs the whole UI update pass twice (the state-only put and the follow-up state+cover put delivered the same state to every screen twice).
- **Progress ring reset animation restored**: the ring once again sweeps back smoothly on a track change instead of snapping to zero - the snap had been introduced on the mistaken theory that the sweep was the perceived lag.
- **Settings and button/menu config changed on the phone only reached the watch after interacting with it**: a watch setting (e.g. album-art blur), a button mapping, or the action-menu list edited on the phone would apply on the watch only after tapping the screen or turning the crown. Root cause: the phone pushed those DataItems to the Data Layer *non-urgently*, so the system batched them for power and could delay the sync by minutes - until unrelated urgent traffic (a control message sent by interacting with the watch) flushed the queue. Music state and notifications were already sent urgent; the button-config, action-list and preference pushes now are too, so they sync immediately. Complementing this, a manifest-registered `ConfigListenerService` applies an incoming button/menu-config change to an already-open now-playing screen even when the phone connection had gone idle, so it lands with no interaction needed.

## 1.12

Wear OS modernization initiative — bringing the watch app up to current platform standards (foundation, system integration, native surfaces) and starting the move to Jetpack Compose. Full roadmap in `docs/wear-modernization-plan.md`.

### Phase 0 — Foundation (wear)

- Target SDK bumped 30 → 34, with the platform changes that become enforced at that target handled so behavior is unchanged on current devices: explicit `android:exported` on the launcher activity + Data Layer listener services (Android 12), and a `mediaPlayback` `foregroundServiceType` + permission on `WatchMusicService` (Android 14).
- Re-enabled the `ExpiredTargetSdkVersion` lint (its suppression is now obsolete).
- Migrated the deprecated `AmbientModeSupport` to `AmbientLifecycleObserver`.
- Removed the dead legacy `support.wearable` `ConfirmationActivity` manifest entry (the androidx one was already in use).
- Declares + requests `POST_NOTIFICATIONS` (Android 13) so the foreground notification keeps showing.

### Phase 1 — System media integration (wear)

- New watch-side **MediaSession proxy** (`WatchMediaSession`): mirrors the phone's now-playing state (title/artist/art/position/playback + remote volume) and forwards transport controls back to the phone. The phone's playback now appears in and is controllable from the system **Media Controls** app and the Wear OS media surfaces — no app UI rewrite required.
- MediaSession flags and `setSessionActivity` now set so the Wear OS recents screen shows the currently playing track name under the app name.
- New watch→phone skip-next / skip-previous command channel (previously only toggle/seek/volume/quick-action existed).
- `WatchMusicService`'s foreground notification is now a MediaStyle notification bound to the session.

### Performance (wear)

- Cut control latency: every watch→phone command was re-resolving the phone node via a `getConnectedNodes()` round-trip on each press. The node id is now cached and reused, so button presses reach the phone noticeably faster.

### Queue redesign (wear)

- Introduced **Jetpack Compose for Wear OS** into the module (first Compose here; pilot for the broader UI modernization).
- Fully replaced the legacy `WearableDrawerLayout` queue with a new **`QueueActivity`** hosting a Compose `QueueScreen`:
  - `ScalingLazyColumn` of dark glass pills; the now-playing entry is highlighted with the album's lightened (pastel) accent colour.
  - Animated three-bar **equalizer** next to the playing track.
  - **Marquee** scrolling for long titles inside the pill.
  - Clock rendered as **`CurvedText`** along the top bezel, matching the Wear OS style; it fades out as the user scrolls down.
  - Thin curved **scroll indicator** on the right bezel — fixed thumb size (no erratic resize with the rotary crown), auto-hides 1.2 s after scrolling stops.
  - **Swipe-to-dismiss** closes only the queue (reveals the now-playing screen underneath); the system window animation is suppressed so the Compose transition plays cleanly without a double-close flash.
  - Google Sans used throughout to match the rest of the watch UI.
- Artist name on the now-playing screen and in the quick-actions panel now uses a HSL-lightened version of the album accent (dark colours, e.g. deep purple, become a readable pastel; black text always used in the queue).

### Bug fixes (mobile + wear)

- **Shuffle button always appeared active**: apps that never set their shuffle mode report `SHUFFLE_MODE_INVALID (-1)` which is not `SHUFFLE_MODE_NONE (0)`, so the comparison wrongly treated them as "shuffling". Fixed by checking for the explicitly-ON states (`ALL` / `GROUP`) instead.
- **Repeat button skipped "repeat one" on some apps** (e.g. Retro Music): `REPEAT_MODE_GROUP` is semantically "repeat all" but was falling through to the `else → NONE` branch in `RepeatAction`, bypassing repeat-one. Fixed.
- **Album art missing on Retro Music and other apps**: many apps on Android 10+ provide art as a `content://` URI rather than a raw `Bitmap` to reduce memory usage. Added URI fallback (`ALBUM_ART_URI` / `ART_URI` / `DISPLAY_ICON_URI`) with synchronous `ContentResolver` loading (network URIs are skipped to avoid blocking the main thread).
- **Like / favourite button not reflecting state on watch after toggling**: some apps don't immediately re-publish their playback state after handling a custom like action. A forced re-read of the state is now scheduled 500 ms after every like action so the watch button updates even in that case.
